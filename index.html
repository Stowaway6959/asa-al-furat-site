The Story

A Cup of Tea
That Never Left Home

When Imtihan Alaid was 11 years old, ISIS invaded Mosul. Her family fled, carrying only what they could, first to Turkey, then eventually to Springfield, Missouri. Through every move, her mother Manal's daily cup of tea with cardamom was the one constant. It smelled like home.

After being laid off in January 2025, Imtihan started selling traditional Iraqi desserts at the C-Street City Market. Springfield had never tasted anything like it. The line was always long.

"Everything is handmade fresh daily. These are recipes we carried out of Iraq." As featured in 417 Magazine

On September 8, 2025, Asa Al-Furat (عصا الفرات) opened at 207 Park Central E — the only authentic Iraqi bakery and coffee shop in Springfield, possibly in all of the Ozarks. The name means The Cane of the Euphrates, a nod to the river and the memories that stretch from Mosul to Missouri.

Imtihan prepares every recipe by hand, quietly and with love. She wants her cafe to be a soft, comforting moment in your day — a small, welcoming chance to discover the true taste of Iraq. As she puts it: my small place, my big culture, and my passion in every piece.

"I want this space to feel soft and comforting, a place that eases the heart, and feels like home — even if it isn't your home." Imtihan Alaid, Founder & Owner
